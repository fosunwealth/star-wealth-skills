# 06 · Display panels

Heavy assets, a strong cycle, and a technology shift. Price is the one variable that dominates.

## Character

- One high-generation line costs tens of billions of yuan. Depreciation is the largest cost.
- Price wars clear the industry. Each trough has an exit or a takeover.

`[extension]` Depreciation is fixed, so utilization sets the unit cost. When price falls, the new line with the heaviest depreciation loses cash first. List each company's line, depreciation life, start year, and what is left.

## What to study

| Point | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Price | The monthly panel-price report from WitsView, Sigmaintell, and Omdia is the core series |
| Capacity | Korean exits (Samsung, LG Display) and new lines in China (BOE, TCL CSOT) |
| Technology | LCD, then OLED, then MiniLED, then MicroLED. Each shift changes content value |
| Demand | TV units, larger average size, commercial, auto, and VR |

## Minimum dashboard

| Dimension | Metric | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | LCD TV panel price by size, OLED panel price | Monthly (WitsView, Sigmaintell, Omdia) |
| Supply | Capacity by generation, Korean shutdowns, China ramp | Quarterly |
| Demand | TV, monitor, and notebook units, average size in inches | Monthly or quarterly |
| Profit | Cash cost per square meter versus the panel price, at the leader | Quarterly |

A trough looks like this: panel prices stable for 2–3 months, shutdown news from a Korean or second-tier plant, and a deliberate utilization cut.
