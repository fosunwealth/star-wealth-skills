# 02 · From the industry down to the company

Three layers: room and cycle, then the chain and the structure, then the company. Items marked `[extension]` were added beyond the source handbook.

## 2.1 Life cycle and TAM

### Place the life cycle

| Stage | Penetration | Character | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 0–10% | Immature technology, high cost, few players | Route, use case, the leader |
| Growth | 10–60% | Penetration rising, both sides busy | Growth, share, capacity |
| Mature | 60–90% | Slower growth, a stable structure | Cost, dividends, the cycle |
| Decline | above 90% | Demand shrinks, capacity exits | The substitute, the exit pace |

`[extension]` Penetration is the only ruler. Define the denominator first (all devices, or only the reachable ones). Studying cost control in the growth stage, or the technology route in the mature stage, spends the effort in the wrong place.

### Three ways to size the market

| Method | How | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top down | Downstream market × penetration × content per unit | AI servers = global server units × AI-server penetration × average price |
| Bottom up | Add the main customers' purchase plans | HBM = NVIDIA + AMD + Google + Amazon + Microsoft |
| Analogy | A similar product's penetration curve | The smartphone curve as a guide for VR/AR or the intelligent car |

Do not believe a company's "hundreds of billions" slide. Many TAMs add in markets the company cannot reach. The reachable market can be a tenth of the slide.

`[extension]` Use two methods. If top-down and bottom-up differ by more than 50%, one assumption is wrong. Find it before you conclude.

## 2.2 Who keeps the profit

Draw the chain first: players, share, gross margin, and capex on every link. You will see who earns the money.

```
materials and equipment → manufacturing, OSAT, modules → brand → channel → buyer
```

| Link type | What they earn | Typical names | Gross margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High barrier | The large profit | ASML, NVIDIA, TSMC advanced nodes | 50%+ |
| Heavy assets | The cycle | Panels, memory, mature-node foundry | 40%+ at the peak, a possible loss at the trough |
| Assembly | The hard margin | Foxconn, Luxshare, and peers | Usually 10–20%, from scale and efficiency |
| Brand | The brand premium | Apple 40%+; Android brands 20–30%; white-label under 10% | See the left |

| Question | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Concentration | CR3 and CR5. Is CR3 above 70% (an oligopoly) or is it open? |
| Barriers | Technology, capital, customer qualification, ecosystem? |
| Bargaining | Against suppliers, against customers, and is there a substitute? |
| Entrants | A large outsider (Apple into cars, Google into chips)? |

`[extension]` The minimum chain deliverable is one table: link, players, share, gross margin, capex over the last 12 months, bargaining direction, barrier type. No table means the chain is not understood.

## 2.3 The company, on four axes

Picking a company is picking the winner.

### Technology

- R&D ratio. A semiconductor design company is often 15–30%. Under 10% is a warning.
- Patents: the ones that matter, not the count.
- Cadence: on the industry's clock, or ahead of it?
- Who leads the technology, and have they worked at a scaled company?

### Customers

- Inside a top chain (Apple, NVIDIA, Huawei, Tesla)?
- One customer above 50% cuts both ways: it shows capability, and it is a risk.
- Share inside that chain, up or down, is the leading indicator.

### Cost and manufacturing

- Gross margin versus peers, and why (technology, scale, or execution).
- Utilization. For a heavy-asset company, under 70% usually means a loss.
- Yield. On an advanced node or an advanced package, one point of yield is real money.

### Management

- Technical or commercial background, and how well they know the industry.
- Integrity, a fraud history, repeated disposals.
- Whether the equity incentives are real.

`[extension]` Score each axis 1–5 with one sentence of evidence. An axis at 2 or below is named as a risk in the conclusion. An integrity or fraud history on management is a veto: stop, and do not discuss valuation (`11-pitfalls.md`).
