# 09 · How to check what is true

Desk work is the start. The gap usually shows up in first-hand checks and in a second source.

## §1 Desk work

The aim is to know enough, before you meet anyone, to ask a question that is worth asking.

| Step | Action | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Prospectus or annual report | The best starting document. Model, product mix, customers and suppliers, risk factors, industry section |
| 2 | Three years of filings and the calls | What management said, whether it happened, whether the story stayed consistent |
| 3 | Several long broker notes | Industry frame, then the company, then the short comments. Do not read only one house |
| 4 | Draw the chain | Upstream, downstream, competitors, share |
| 5 | List the open questions | What you do not understand, what conflicts, what has to be checked. That list is the fieldwork |

Five questions:

1. What does the company do, and how does it get paid?
2. What drove the last three years?
3. What is the industry's core tension, and where is the cycle?
4. What is the consensus, and where can it be wrong?
5. What has to be checked in the field?

`[extension]` Question 4 is the hinge (`01-cognition.md`). No answer means desk work is not finished, and fieldwork does not start.

## §2 Chain checks

This is where expectation gaps usually come from.

| Order | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Suppliers | Most objective. They see who is taking more and who is cutting |
| 2 | Customers | Most concrete. Volume, the product, the next plan |
| 3 | Competitors | Most willing to name the other side's weak points |
| 4 | Management | Last. You can only tell a fact from a script after you know the industry |

An important conclusion needs 2–3 independent sources. One source, especially the company, gets a question mark.

Ask:

- Are this year's orders up or down versus last year, and by about how much?
- Whose share is rising, whose is falling, and why?
- Is the price up or down lately, and what is next?
- What is utilization, and how many months is the build plan?
- Is there a new technology, and who is ahead?
- Are customers paying, or are receivables slipping?

Compliance: do not ask for non-public earnings, unannounced orders, or unannounced deals. This is industry work. Conclusions rest on public information and industry common sense.

## §3 Expert interviews

A fast channel, and an easy place to be wrong.

Before: finish the desk work. Do not ask for basics. Order the questions from open to specific. Learn the background: company, role, years, specialty.

While asking: ask how and why, not a leading yes/no. Ask the same point two or three ways. Ask a small question, not "how do you see the industry". Ask for an example and a number.

After: one expert has a position, a blind spot, and can be wrong. Ask people with different backgrounds. Compare what they said with public data and with the chain check. Mark the conflicts and keep checking.

Four traps: treating the expert as an oracle; a question that is too big; hearing only what you hoped for; concluding from one expert.

## Confidence

| Level | Condition | Can it be a conclusion? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | At least 3 independent sources agree, and at least 1 is a supplier or a customer | Yes, as a core claim |
| Medium | 2 independent sources agree | Yes, and say how far it was checked |
| Low | One source, or only the company | No. It stays an open item |
| Conflict | Sources disagree | The conflict is the information. Disclose it and keep checking |
