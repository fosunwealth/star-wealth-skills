# 11 · Traps

The chapter to reread.

## 6.1 Five cognitive traps

| # | Trap | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Straight-line extrapolation | The most common error. A good patch is treated as permanent and given a high value. A bad patch is treated as permanent and given a low one. Hardware cycles. Nothing grows forever, and nothing stays down forever |
| 2 | Technology worship | The best spec sheet is treated as the best investment. Cost, customers, channel, and management matter as much. Many strong technical companies never commercialize |
| 3 | A story without orders | "Domestic substitution", "AI", or "a new technology" is believed without an order, revenue, or a date for profit. The story ends, and nothing is left |
| 4 | Overconfidence | Two weeks of work, then a large conclusion. Hardware is deep. Many founders cannot see their own future clearly |
| 5 | Ignored downside | Only the upside is written. Before a positive view: if this is wrong, how much, and on what fact? |

`[extension]` Trap 1 and "do not call the top of AI hardware" (`05-ai-compute.md`) do not conflict. What is forbidden is extending today's prosperity into future earnings and then into a valuation. What is allowed is judging whether the cycle continues from the change at the margin in the downstream capex guide. One uses the past and the present. The other uses orders and commitments about the future.

`[extension]` For trap 5, every conclusion carries "if this is wrong": two or three falsifiers, observable and dated, and a downside. A positive view with no falsifier does not stand.

## 6.2 Seven fraud signals

| # | Signal | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Gross margin far above peers | More than 10 points, for a sustained period, with no reason that holds (technology, or mix). Probably wrong |
| 2 | Lots of cash and lots of interest-bearing debt | Why borrow at a high rate if the cash is real? |
| 3 | Receivables or inventory growing much faster than revenue | Revenue +20%, receivables +50%, inventory +60%. Often channel stuffing |
| 4 | Operating cash flow divorced from net income | The income statement looks fine. The cash does not. Paper profit |
| 5 | Auditor changes | Especially a large firm to a small one. The auditor often found something and left |
| 6 | Large pledges or disposals by the controlling holder | Especially a large sale at a high price. The trade is the honest signal |
| 7 | A high share of related-party sales | Revenue and profit made with affiliates. No real third-party customer |

Hardware is manufacturing. Numbers that look too good usually are not real.

`[extension]` Tick all seven.

- 1 hit: an item to explain.
- 2 hits: a material risk, named in the conclusion.
- 3 or more: stop. Do not discuss valuation or upside.
